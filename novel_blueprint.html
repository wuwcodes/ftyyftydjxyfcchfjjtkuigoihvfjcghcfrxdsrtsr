Poor Dad's Home — Hawaii
This is the childhood home in Hawaii where Robert grew up watching his educated father struggle with money despite a good salary. It is where Robert first noticed the stark contrast between his two father figures and began questioning everything he had been taught about work, money, and success.

This is Rich Dad's workplace in Hawaii where nine-year-old Robert was put to work stacking canned goods for no pay. This represents the broader world of traditional employment that Rich Dad warns Robert about throughout the book. It is where most people trade their time for a paycheck, pay taxes first, and never build real wealth — the system both dads navigated, but approached with completely opposite mindsets and results.

Why "Mindset"?

The entire book is not really about money — it is about how you think about money. Robert's poor dad had a salary, an education, and a stable job, but a scarcity mindset. His rich dad had a growth mindset: he saw opportunities where others saw obstacles, assets where others saw expenses.

The same dollar, the same hour, the same paycheck leads to completely different outcomes depending solely on how you think about it. Kiyosaki's core argument is that financial literacy begins in the mind — not the bank account.

The color choices reflect this: green for growth and financial literacy, gold for the wealth that follows when the mindset changes. Together they represent what happens when thinking shifts.

"The single most powerful asset we all have is our mind. If it is trained well, it can create enormous wealth."

— Rich Dad Poor Dad, p. 87
