Unit Tier List / 02

Ranked by exam weight. ★★★ S-tier = study FIRST. Together S+A units = ~75% of your score.

UNIT 7 · NATURAL SELECTIONEvolution + Hardy-Weinberg
EXAM WEIGHT: 13–20% · highest single unit
★★★ S-TIER
  • Natural selection = differential survival/reproduction based on heritable traits. NOT "strongest survives" — best fit for the environment.
  • Evidence for evolution: fossils, homologous structures (same origin, diff function — bat wing/whale flipper), vestigial structures, embryology, biogeography, molecular (DNA/protein similarity).
  • 5 ways evolution happens: natural selection, genetic drift (random, big effect in small pops), gene flow (migration), mutation, non-random mating.
  • Hardy-Weinberg = NO EVOLUTION. Conditions: large pop, no mutation, no migration, no selection, random mating. If any one fails → evolution.
  • Speciation: allopatric (geographic barrier), sympatric (same area, e.g. polyploidy in plants).
  • Phylogenetic trees: shared branch points = common ancestor. Closer branches = more recent common ancestor. Don't read "across" the tips.
  • Origin of life: RNA world hypothesis (RNA = info + catalyst), Miller-Urey (organic molecules from inorganic).
Amoeba: Natural Selection Amoeba: Hardy-Weinberg Bozeman: Natural Selection
Common trapHardy-Weinberg: q² is the homozygous RECESSIVE frequency, not q. If 16% show recessive trait → q² = 0.16 → q = 0.4 → p = 0.6.
UNIT 3 · CELLULAR ENERGETICSPhotosynthesis + Respiration + Enzymes
EXAM WEIGHT: 12–16%
★★★ S-TIER
  • Enzymes lower activation energy. Specific to substrate (lock & key / induced fit). Affected by temp, pH, [substrate], inhibitors (competitive vs non-competitive/allosteric).
  • Photosynthesis: 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + light → C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂. Two stages:
    · Light reactions (thylakoid): water split, makes ATP + NADPH + O₂.
    · Calvin cycle (stroma): uses ATP + NADPH to fix CO₂ into G3P → glucose. NO LIGHT NEEDED directly.
  • Cellular respiration (reverse): C₆H₁₂O₆ + 6O₂ → 6CO₂ + 6H₂O + ATP. Stages:
    · Glycolysis (cytoplasm): glucose → 2 pyruvate, net 2 ATP. Anaerobic.
    · Krebs/Citric Acid (matrix): pyruvate → CO₂, makes NADH/FADH₂.
    · ETC + chemiosmosis (inner membrane): electrons drive H⁺ pumping → H⁺ flows back through ATP synthase → ~32 ATP. O₂ is final electron acceptor.
  • Fermentation = no O₂. Regenerates NAD⁺ so glycolysis continues. Lactic acid (animals) or ethanol+CO₂ (yeast).
  • Chemiosmosis = the universal trick. Both photosynthesis and respiration use a proton gradient across a membrane to make ATP via ATP synthase.
Amoeba: Photosynthesis Amoeba: Cell Respiration Amoeba: Enzymes
Common trapPhotosynthesis ≠ "the opposite" of respiration in cells. PLANTS DO BOTH. They photosynthesize in light AND respire 24/7 in mitochondria.
UNIT 6 · GENE EXPRESSION & REGULATIONDNA → RNA → Protein
EXAM WEIGHT: 12–16%
★★★ S-TIER
  • Central dogma: DNA → (transcription) → mRNA → (translation) → protein.
  • DNA replication: semiconservative. Helicase unwinds, primase lays RNA primer, DNA polymerase adds nucleotides 5'→3'. Leading strand continuous, lagging strand = Okazaki fragments. Ligase seals.
  • Transcription (nucleus, eukaryotes): RNA polymerase reads DNA template → mRNA. Then mRNA processing: 5' cap, poly-A tail, splicing out introns (keep exons).
  • Translation (ribosome): mRNA codons read 3 at a time. tRNA brings amino acids (anticodon matches codon). Start: AUG (methionine). Stop codons: UAA, UAG, UGA.
  • Mutations: point (silent / missense / nonsense), frameshift (insertion/deletion shifts reading frame — usually catastrophic).
  • Gene regulation in bacteria: lac operon (inducible — ON when lactose present), trp operon (repressible — OFF when tryptophan abundant).
  • Eukaryote regulation: transcription factors, enhancers, epigenetics (DNA methylation = silenced; histone acetylation = active).
  • Biotech: PCR (amplifies DNA, cycles of denaturing/annealing/extending), gel electrophoresis (separates by size — small fragments travel farther), CRISPR (gene editing).
Amoeba: DNA Replication Amoeba: Protein Synthesis Amoeba: Gene Regulation/Operon
Common trap"Codon" is on mRNA. "Anticodon" is on tRNA. They are COMPLEMENTARY (e.g., codon AUG ↔ anticodon UAC). Also: U replaces T in RNA.
UNIT 2 · CELL STRUCTURE & FUNCTIONMembranes + Transport
EXAM WEIGHT: 10–13%
★★ A-TIER
  • Plasma membrane = phospholipid bilayer (hydrophilic heads out, hydrophobic tails in) + proteins + cholesterol. Fluid mosaic model.
  • Passive transport (no ATP): diffusion, osmosis (water across membrane), facilitated diffusion (through channel/carrier protein).
  • Active transport (uses ATP): pumps molecules against gradient. Sodium-potassium pump = classic. Endocytosis/exocytosis for big stuff.
  • Tonicity: hypertonic (more solute outside) → water leaves cell → shrivels. Hypotonic → water enters → swells/lyses. Isotonic → no net.
  • Surface area : volume — small cells win. As cell grows, volume outpaces SA → can't get nutrients in/waste out. Why cells stay small.
  • Organelles to know: nucleus (DNA), mitochondria (ATP, double membrane), chloroplast (photosynthesis, double membrane), ER (rough = ribosomes/protein, smooth = lipids), Golgi (modify/ship), lysosome (digest), ribosome (translate).
  • Endosymbiotic theory: mitochondria & chloroplasts were once free-living prokaryotes. Evidence: own DNA, own ribosomes, double membrane, divide independently.
Amoeba: Cell Transport Amoeba: Osmosis
Common trapWater moves toward higher solute / lower water potential. Memorize: "salty side sucks." Water always chases the salt.
UNIT 5 · HEREDITYMendel + Punnett + Pedigrees
EXAM WEIGHT: 8–11%
★★ A-TIER
  • Mitosis = body cells, 2 identical diploid daughters (2n→2n). For growth/repair.
  • Meiosis = gametes, 4 unique haploid daughters (2n→n). Two divisions. Source of genetic variation: crossing over (prophase I), independent assortment (metaphase I), random fertilization.
  • Mendel's laws: segregation (alleles separate in gametes), independent assortment (genes on different chromosomes inherited independently).
  • Punnett basics: Aa × Aa → 1 AA : 2 Aa : 1 aa = 3:1 phenotype if A is dominant.
  • Non-Mendelian: incomplete dominance (red × white = pink), codominance (both shown, AB blood), multiple alleles (ABO blood: I^A, I^B, i), polygenic (height/skin), pleiotropy (one gene, many effects).
  • Sex-linked (X-linked recessive): males more affected (only one X). Color blindness, hemophilia.
  • Pedigree shortcut: two unaffected parents with affected kid → recessive. Affected parent every generation → dominant. Mostly males affected, skips generations → X-linked recessive.
  • Chi-square (χ²) tests if observed ratios match expected. If χ² > critical value → reject null → ratios don't match.
Amoeba: Mitosis Amoeba: Meiosis Amoeba: Punnett Squares
Common trapMitosis vs Meiosis: mitosis has ONE division, meiosis has TWO. Crossing over only happens in meiosis I.
UNIT 8 · ECOLOGYPopulations + Energy + Ecosystems
EXAM WEIGHT: 10–15%
★★ A-TIER
  • Trophic levels: producers → primary consumers → secondary → tertiary. Only ~10% of energy passes up each level (rest lost as heat). That's why food chains are short.
  • Population growth:
    · Exponential (J-curve): unlimited resources, dN/dt = rN.
    · Logistic (S-curve): hits carrying capacity K. dN/dt = rN(K−N)/K.
  • r vs K-selected: r = many offspring, little care (insects, fish). K = few offspring, lots of care (humans, elephants).
  • Symbiosis: mutualism (++), commensalism (+0), parasitism (+−), predation (+−), competition (−−).
  • Biogeochemical cycles: carbon (photosynthesis ↔ respiration ↔ combustion), nitrogen (N₂ → NH₃ by bacteria — fixation), water, phosphorus (no atmospheric phase).
  • Biodiversity = stability. Higher diversity → ecosystem resists disturbance. Simpson's Diversity Index measures it.
  • Human impact: climate change, invasive species, habitat destruction, eutrophication (excess nutrients → algal bloom → dead zones).

UNIT 4 · CELL COMMUNICATION & CELL CYCLESignaling + Mitosis Regulation
EXAM WEIGHT: 10–15%
★ B-TIER
  • Signal transduction = 3 steps: RECEPTION (signal binds receptor) → TRANSDUCTION (relay, often phosphorylation cascade) → RESPONSE (gene expression / protein activity).
  • Receptor types: G-protein coupled, receptor tyrosine kinase, ligand-gated ion channel, intracellular (for steroids — they cross membrane).
  • Feedback: negative (most common — output reduces input, e.g. body temp, blood sugar). Positive (amplifies — childbirth oxytocin, blood clotting).
  • Cell cycle: G1 → S (DNA replicated) → G2 → M. Checkpoints at G1, G2, M ensure DNA isn't damaged before proceeding.
  • Cancer = loss of cell cycle control. Mutations in proto-oncogenes (gas pedal) or tumor suppressors like p53 (brakes).
  • Apoptosis = programmed cell death. Removes damaged or unneeded cells (e.g., webbing between fingers in development).

UNIT 1 · CHEMISTRY OF LIFEWater + Macromolecules
EXAM WEIGHT: 8–11%
SKIM
  • Water properties (all from polarity / hydrogen bonding): cohesion (water-water), adhesion (water-other), high specific heat, high heat of vaporization, less dense as solid (ice floats), universal solvent.
  • 4 macromolecules:
    · Carbs (energy/structure): monomer = monosaccharide (glucose). Polymers = starch, glycogen, cellulose.
    · Lipids (energy/membranes): NOT polymers. Triglycerides (saturated/unsaturated), phospholipids, steroids.
    · Proteins (everything): monomer = amino acid. Peptide bonds. 4 levels of structure (1° sequence → 2° helix/sheet → 3° 3D fold → 4° multiple subunits).
    · Nucleic acids: monomer = nucleotide (sugar + phosphate + base). DNA = double helix, deoxyribose, ATCG. RNA = single, ribose, AUCG.

  • Protein denaturation: high temp / extreme pH disrupts H-bonds → loss of 3D shape → loss of function.

Formula Strip / 03

You GET a formula sheet on exam day. But you have to know when to use each one. These are the high-frequency ones.

Hardy-Weinberg

p² + 2pq + q² = 1
p + q = 1

p = dominant allele freq, q = recessive. q² = % showing recessive trait. 2pq = heterozygous freq.

Chi-Square (χ²)

χ² = Σ (O − E)² / E

Tests if your observed data matches expected. Compare to critical value at df = categories − 1, p = 0.05. If χ² > critical → reject null.

Water Potential

Ψ = Ψp + Ψs
Ψs = −iCRT

Water moves from high Ψ to low Ψ. In open container, Ψp = 0, so Ψ = Ψs. i = ions (sucrose = 1, NaCl = 2).

Population Growth

Exponential: dN/dt = rN
Logistic: dN/dt = rN(K−N)/K

K = carrying capacity. When N = K, growth = 0 (logistic). r = max growth rate.

Simpson's Diversity

D = 1 − Σ(n/N)²

n = # of one species, N = total. Higher D = more diverse.

Surface Area : Volume

Sphere: SA = 4πr², V = (4/3)πr³

As r grows, V grows faster than SA. Small cells = high SA:V ratio = better exchange.

Active Recall Flip Cards / 04

Tap each card. Try to answer BEFORE opening it. This is the highest-leverage thing you can do tonight.

Q: Difference between mitosis and meiosis?
Mitosis: 1 division → 2 identical diploid (2n) cells, for growth/repair. Meiosis: 2 divisions → 4 unique haploid (n) gametes, with crossing over + independent assortment for variation.
Q: What does ATP synthase actually do?
It's a protein channel that lets H⁺ ions flow DOWN their gradient across a membrane. The flow physically spins it, and that spinning catalyzes ADP + Pi → ATP. Used in both mitochondria (respiration) and chloroplasts (photosynthesis).
Q: A population has q² = 0.36. What's p?
q = √0.36 = 0.6. So p = 1 − 0.6 = 0.4. Heterozygous frequency = 2pq = 2(0.4)(0.6) = 0.48.
Q: Why is the lagging strand "lagging"?
DNA polymerase only adds nucleotides 5'→3'. The lagging strand template runs the wrong way, so synthesis happens in short Okazaki fragments, away from the replication fork. Ligase glues them together later.
Q: What's the final electron acceptor in cellular respiration? Photosynthesis?
Respiration: O₂ (becomes water). Photosynthesis: NADP⁺ (becomes NADPH).
Q: Cell placed in hypertonic solution — what happens?
Solution has more solute outside. Water rushes OUT of cell → cell shrivels (animal) or plasmolyzes (plant). Remember: "salty side sucks."
Q: How do you know if a trait is X-linked recessive from a pedigree?
Mostly males affected, skips generations, can pass from carrier mother to son. Affected females must have affected father AND carrier/affected mother.
Q: 3 ways prokaryotes regulate genes via the operon model?
(1) Inducible operon (lac) — normally OFF, turns ON when substrate present. (2) Repressible operon (trp) — normally ON, turns OFF when product abundant. (3) Repressors and activators bind operator/promoter regions.
Q: Why are food chains rarely longer than 4–5 levels?
Only ~10% of energy transfers up each trophic level (rest lost as heat). After 4–5 levels, there's not enough energy left to support another consumer.
Q: 3 evidences mitochondria came from prokaryotes?
(1) Own circular DNA. (2) Own 70S ribosomes (like bacteria). (3) Double membrane. (4) Divide by binary fission. Endosymbiotic theory.
Q: What does p53 do, and what happens if it mutates?
p53 is a tumor suppressor — it halts the cell cycle when DNA is damaged, triggers repair, or signals apoptosis. If mutated, damaged cells divide unchecked → cancer.
Q: Negative vs positive feedback — give an example of each.
Negative: response REDUCES the stimulus (body temperature, blood glucose via insulin/glucagon). Positive: response AMPLIFIES the stimulus (childbirth oxytocin, blood clotting).

FRQ Tactics / 05

FRQs are 50% of your score. Most students lose points not from being wrong but from being vague. Here's how AP Readers actually grade.

  1. READ THE COMMAND VERB. "Describe" = list features. "Explain" = give mechanism + cause/effect. "Justify" = use data as evidence. "Predict" = give an outcome AND why. Wrong verb = no points.
  2. Answer in the same language as the question. If the question says "natural selection," your answer should literally say "natural selection." Don't dance around terms.
  3. Always connect structure → function. "Cristae increase surface area, which holds more ETC proteins, which makes more ATP." That's 3 points instead of 1.
  4. For experimental design FRQs: identify independent variable, dependent variable, a control group, and at least one variable held constant. Then state your hypothesis in "if/then" form.
  5. For graph FRQs: label both axes WITH UNITS. Title it. Use the right type (bar = categories, line = continuous). Plot data points clearly.
  6. For data analysis: first describe the trend ("as X increases, Y also increases"). Then explain WHY biologically. Then connect to a bigger concept.
  7. If you don't know an answer: still write something. Use vocabulary from the question. Partial credit exists. Blank = guaranteed zero.
  8. Time: 90 min for 6 FRQs. ~15 min each. The 2 long FRQs are worth more (8–10 pts) than the 4 short ones (4 pts). Plan accordingly.
